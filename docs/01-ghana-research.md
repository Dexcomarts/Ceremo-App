# Ghana Market and Customer Research

Version 3.4 · Revised 5 November 2026. Initial evidence gathered 19–20 October 2026; source-specific dates and retrieval limits remain attached to the findings.

## 1. The opportunity

CEREMO's commercial opportunity is to make provider selection useful enough for organisers to return and for providers to fund discovery through subscriptions. Greater Accra is the initial service area; the catalogue supports all occasions and festivals. Customer discovery is free, with service discussions, contracts and payments permitted outside the application.

The investment case rests on two unvalidated propositions: organisers gain a better route to suitable providers than their existing alternatives, and providers receive incremental value at an acceptable subscription cost. Cultural activity and internet access establish context; neither proves these propositions.

| Decision question | Evidence currently available | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the service need credible? | Cultural/academic sources describe varied occasions and coordination across service categories. | Include the occasion families and service-specific requirements; validate actual sourcing difficulty. |
| Is the market unserved? | Public Ghana directories and event platforms already advertise relevant offers. | Compete on demonstrated usefulness rather than claims to be the first directory. |
| Does outside contact undermine the model? | Subscription-funded provider platforms provide a commercial precedent; CEREMO permits direct arrangements. | Measure useful demand and renewal rather than forcing every conversation/payment back into the app. |
| Will Ghanaian providers pay? | No validated local price, trial duration or renewal benchmark has been established. | Test a disclosed real offer; stated interest and free registrations are insufficient. |
| Is expansion justified? | No CEREMO interview, commercial pilot or national demand result is recorded. | Validate category/locality coverage and operating economics in Greater Accra first. |

The [PRD](02-product-requirements.md) defines product decisions and release gates. This research supplies their evidence, limits and proposed tests. It does not establish a market valuation, fraud prevalence, proprietary advantage or product-market fit.

## 2. Ceremonies and occasions

### Marriage ceremonies

Accra Metropolitan Assembly lists ordinance, customary and Mohammedan marriage registration. The Registrar General describes ordinance marriage as a secular civil union. Legal marriage form, religious setting and reception arrangements are therefore separate questions. [AMA marriage registration](https://www.ama.gov.gh/marriage-registration.php), [Registrar General marriage guidance](https://www.rgd.gov.gh/marriage.html).

A couple may arrange several sessions, with different dates, locations and providers. Discovery should accommodate their actual service needs without requiring a fixed ceremony sequence. A provider listing does not establish that an officiant is legally authorised or suitable for the family's religious/customary arrangements. Registration procedures should be checked with the responsible registry.

### Naming ceremonies and funerals

Adjah's article describes naming practices and eighth-day outdooring. It was published in 2011, although Cambridge's online publication date is 2022. CEREMO should include naming/outdooring and ask for the family's actual date, without imposing an eighth-day rule or requiring a child's personal details for an ordinary service enquiry. [Adjah, Ghanaian personal names](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/african-research-and-documentation/article/abs/what-is-in-a-name-ghanaian-personal-names-as-information-sources/9B8993E767AEC67D9D7E0AA13BF27D01).

A 2018 study examines two chiefly funeral posters in Ewe communities in Ghana and Togo. It describes disagreements about names and kinship. The small study cannot describe every Ghanaian funeral, but it gives a practical reason to name the person responsible for approving printed programmes, announcements and captions. Funeral enquiries also need careful handling of family information. [Adotey, funeral posters](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/06a64fff-0045-41ff-916a-4a80030ebad0).

Official catalogues can disagree. The NCC's Damba month heading and the tourism page's lunar description should not become an automatic calendar rule. Community accounts of historical events also need attribution as tradition. Festival dates, access and authority to organise activities require local confirmation.

These occasions may require catering, tents, sanitation, sound, photography and transport. The existence of a festival does not establish how much of that work is available to a marketplace. Interviews should distinguish public festival services, private related gatherings and ceremonial activities that require separate permission.

### Local restrictions

The Greater Accra Regional Peace Council's statement dated 31 March 2026 relays a drumming/noise ban from 4 May to 4 June 2026 in affected Ga Traditional Council jurisdictions. It addresses instruments, loudspeakers and gatherings. Those dates are specific to 2026 and do not describe every part of Greater Accra. [Statement](https://www.peacecouncil.gov.gh/2026/03/garpc-statement-on-the-ban-on-drumming-and-noise-making/), [dated PDF](https://www.peacecouncil.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Homowo-festival-observance-2026.pdf).

CEREMO should store notices with a source, affected area, effective dates and review date. A sound or venue request can then show the relevant notice. The organiser and provider still need to confirm what applies; the application does not issue permits.

## 3. Customers and access

The 2021 census reported an urban population share of 56.7%. Greater Accra and Ashanti accounted for 47.8% of the increase in urban population between 2010 and 2021. This supports investigating a concentrated local launch. It does not measure demand for CEREMO or provider willingness to subscribe; customers remain free. [GSS release, 18 November 2021](https://census2020.statsghana.gov.gh/presspage.php?Presentation-on-the-General-Report-Volumes-3A-3B-and-3C=&readmorenews=MTY4OTA1MDkwNC4wOTY%3D).

GSS's digital exclusion release, published on 28 April 2023 using 2021 census data, reports that 31.4% of people aged six and above had not used the internet in the previous three months. It also reports that 36.7% of adults aged 18 and above had not used a mobile device for a financial transaction in that period. These are historic figures with different age groups. [GSS digital exclusion](https://census2021.statsghana.gov.gh/newspage.php?Release-of-the-2021-PHC-Thematic-Brief-on-Digital-Exclusion-in-Ghana=&readmorenews=MjUwNzE4NDkzNS43Njc1).

For CEREMO, this means testing onboarding with people of different ages and digital experience. The backend should support small responses, safe retries, payment status checks and operational SMS. Assisted provider onboarding is worth testing. The [frontend stack](05-ui-ux-design-brief.md) is selected; visual design remains undecided.

The University of Ghana thesis *The White Wedding in Ghana: Wedding Professionals in Perspective* describes coordination across attire, food, music and photography. It also records a photographer's concern about collecting payment after delivery and the importance of portfolios. Its cover is dated July 2018, with older fieldwork and inconsistent later signature dates. Use it as historical qualitative evidence, not a current price list or a measure of how often problems occur. [Kyei-Sakyi thesis, printed pp. 68–69 and 80–81](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/server/api/core/bitstreams/2102dd21-3528-4e34-a660-996334d95cc6/content).

The current product helps customers compare useful provider information and share a complete brief. Providers and customers agree service and payment terms directly. Whether CEREMO improves discovery and earns provider renewals needs testing.


### Ghana social-commerce evidence

Edward Entee's October 2022 University of Ghana thesis describes a fashion business using Instagram and Facebook for discovery and WhatsApp for customer communication and orders. Its main interview work took place in 2019. This single-business case and its network demonstrate a possible workflow, not national preferences or ceremony-market conversion rates. [Social Commerce and Value Co-Creation](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/server/api/core/bitstreams/cf0bb55d-c494-431a-ad9c-0f6b1b8582fc/content).

A Ghana mobile-phone survey published in 2018 found coverage bias toward male, urban, younger and better-educated respondents compared with household benchmarks. Use several recruitment routes; the study does not supply current CEREMO customer demographics. [L'Engle et al., PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0190902).

GSMA's 3 September 2025 Ghana announcement distinguishes network coverage from actual use and discusses device affordability. It supports investigating device access and digital confidence, not assuming that all Greater Accra organisers use the same tools. The announcement's indexed text was reviewed; the direct page could not be fetched during this review. [GSMA Ghana announcement](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/new-gsma-report-launches-at-digital-africa-summit-ghana-revealing-digital-transformation-could-add-20-billion-to-the-country/).

The working hypothesis is that CEREMO should fit practical conversation habits while improving discovery and the evidence available when choosing a provider. Research must examine who recommends, chooses and pays; these may be different people in a household, committee or organisation. No credible local subscription willingness-to-pay figure, renewal rate or customer lifetime value was established.

## 6. Privacy and operations

Ghana's Data Protection Act includes controller registration, security and processor-contract requirements, compromise notification and direct-marketing rules. Registration renewal is addressed on a two-year basis. CEREMO needs a data inventory, registration arrangements, processor agreements, retention rules and a process for handling personal-data requests before live operation. [Act 843, particularly sections 27–31, 40 and 50](https://dpc.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Data-Protection-Act-2012-Act-843.pdf), [accessible official CSA copy](https://csa.gov.gh/resources/Data_Protection_Act_2012.pdf).

The Bank of Ghana's indexed Act 987 sets out payment-services licensing requirements, and its indexed directory lists Paystack Ghana as PSP Enhanced. Full page retrieval failed for these two sources; only their official search-index content was verified. PSP approval and local legal review should cover CEREMO's actual money flow, especially settlement and refunds. [Act 987](https://www.bog.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Payment-Systems-and-Services-Act-2019-Act-987_.pdf), [licensed PSP directory](https://www.bog.gov.gh/supervision-regulation/registered-institutions/list-of-licensed-psps/).

Hubtel's current portal advertises SMS and notifications. Confirm API access, sender-ID approval and delivery before choosing production endpoints; an older documentation address redirects. Development should use a fake SMS adapter. [Hubtel developer portal](https://developers.hubtel.com/).

GhanaPostGPS describes its digital address grid and the need for accurate location capture at the property. Where the user elects to provide precise access details for a later service discussion, record their purpose and permitted recipient. Initial discovery needs locality rather than an exact private address. A formatted code does not prove ownership or identify the right entrance. No automated GhanaPostGPS API has been selected. [GhanaPostGPS FAQ](https://nas.ghanapostgps.com/get-help/).

| Customer | Provider |
| --- | --- |

| Optional guide with a direct route to ordinary search | Clear trial end, subscription price, benefits and expiry behaviour |

Start the trial when an approved profile is first published, rather than while the provider is waiting for a review. Recommend no payment method requirement and no automatic trial charge initially. Trial duration remains `null`. Prevent repeated trials for the same provider through reviewed account history, without collecting unnecessary identity data.

Recommend monthly manual renewal for launch. Publish the exact price and benefits before payment. An expired trial or paid period stops active discovery/contact entitlement, while the provider retains account management, invoices, support and its records. Historical profile and review information can remain accessible with an inactive label for accountability; expiry must not erase criticism or suggest the business failed verification.

Two initial tier concepts are enough to research: an ordinary listing subscription and a subscription that includes eligible sponsored placement. Their names, prices, quotas and delivery rules remain open. A separate pay-per-enquiry, verification purchase or boost purchase would add another charging model and is not approved by the subscription-only decision.

## 9. Verification, sponsorship and reviews

| Signal | What it means | What it must not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscription | The provider has current access to the purchased features | Identity approval, excellent work or a guaranteed customer |
| Verification check | A stated check passed on a stated date, with expiry where relevant | Every aspect of the provider is trusted or every job will succeed |
| Sponsored | The listing receives commercial placement | It is the highest-rated or most suitable provider |
| Customer rating | Published customer experience ratings, with count and evidence information | A score bought by purchasing a tier |

A subscription may fund a verification assessment. For example, checking a business registration or contact method can have a defined process, reviewer and outcome. A failed assessment must remain failed even if the provider paid. Explain what happens to the subscription if publication requirements are not met before taking payment. Separate assessment fees are not approved under the subscription-only model.

The customer should see meaningful wording such as a completed business-registration check and its date. Broad labels such as "Guaranteed" would promise more than this assessment establishes. Verification evidence stays private.

### Reviews for externally arranged services

An enquiry or contact click does not prove that a service was delivered. Reviews therefore need a different basis from the earlier completed-booking rule.

Allow authenticated customers to describe a genuine experience, state the service and approximate date, and submit one review per actual service experience under a published duplication policy. Distinguish customer-reported experience from evidence reviewed by CEREMO. A selected, redacted agreement, receipt or other appropriate record may support review, but payment screenshots alone do not prove quality or full delivery. Never collect MoMo PINs, OTPs or complete private conversations.

Give providers a right to respond and report suspicious reviews. Their approval must not be required to publish legitimate criticism. Apply the same conflict-of-interest, fake-review and moderation rules regardless of subscription level. Yelp's own guidelines illustrate explicit conflict rules, although CEREMO must define its own policy. [Yelp guidelines](https://www.yelp.com/guidelines).

Subscription expiry should not delete reviews. A provider report of a booking and a customer report can disagree; retain source labels rather than manufacturing a verified outcome. Handling a complaint can lead to profile correction, investigation or suspension without promising that CEREMO can refund an outside service payment.

## 10. Optional onboarding and privacy

Offer a short guide after registration, with **Skip**, **Not sure**, **Back** and **Edit preferences**. Browsing should not repeatedly demand completion. Asking necessary questions, explaining their purpose and retaining answers when moving back follows established question-page guidance. [GOV.UK question pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/question-pages/).

| Question | Use | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are you planning an occasion or exploring ideas? | Choose a useful starting view | Optional; exploring need not create an event |
| Which services do you need? | Select service categories | Multiple selections; ordinary search remains available |
| Where will the service be needed? | Match provider coverage | Locality is sufficient initially; no GPS permission or exact home address required |
| When is it needed? | Help the provider understand timing | Optional date or flexible range; not confirmed availability |
| What is your budget for this service? | Compare relevant price information | Optional GHS range and unit, such as total or per guest; unknown is null, not zero |
| Which providers would you like to save? | Maintain a customer-selected shortlist | Optional; not a signal that those providers were hired |

Keep event-specific answers in that event context. Save broader category preferences only when the customer chooses to do so. A funeral enquiry must not become a permanent bereavement label, and a wedding budget must not become an estimate of wealth. Do not ask for religion or ethnicity to operate ordinary discovery.

Ghana's Act 843 addresses minimality, specified purposes, compatible use and special personal data; section 40 requires prior written consent for direct marketing. DPC guidance also calls for explaining whether answers are optional and how data will be used. Define the lawful basis and show exactly what an enquiry sends to a selected provider. Do not distribute private budgets or interests to every subscriber. [Act 843, sections 19–25, 37 and 40](https://csa.gov.gh/resources/Data_Protection_Act_2012.pdf), [DPC organisation guidance](https://dpc.gov.gh/for-organisations/), [individual rights](https://dpc.gov.gh/for-individuals/).

## 11. Matching and paid visibility

Start with explainable rules, not a machine-learning project. Build the eligible set from approved providers with a current trial or paid entitlement, a published matching service, coverage of the requested locality and an accepting-enquiries state. Suspension overrides payment. Recheck eligibility when opening contact actions or sending an enquiry.

Rank ordinary results by the current category, coverage, applicable price information, profile completeness, response evidence and the strength of published reviews. Define and version the exact ranking policy before release. Unknown prices mean a quote is needed; unknown dates mean availability needs confirmation. Do not advertise these as free or available.

Sponsored candidates must pass the same relevance and publication checks and have the required current subscription benefit. Show a labelled sponsored area, followed by ordinary matching results. There are limited top positions, so use a disclosed allocation rule and stable rotation among eligible candidates. Do not sell every provider a promise to be first whenever someone chooses their category. Avoid duplicate appearances on one result page.

Google's ordinary local results illustrate the distinction: relevance, distance and prominence inform ranking, and the company says ordinary local ranking cannot be bought. Its ad systems have separate commercial rules. CEREMO need not copy Google's algorithm to preserve that distinction. [Google local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en).

Do not require a perfect five-star average by default. A new provider has no customer score, and one five-star review provides different evidence from many reviews. Display the actual average and count. If customers explicitly select a rating filter, honour it and explain empty results. Offer a clear way to broaden criteria rather than silently relaxing them. New eligible providers can receive a limited opportunity to appear without invented reviews.

When no match exists, show that fact and let the customer change category, area or budget. Suggesting another area requires the customer's choice. Do not fill a personalised feed with unrelated subscribers simply to deliver exposure.

## 13. Customer and provider retention

On 5 November 2026, the founder approved registration before full provider profiles, detailed reviews and contact access. Anonymous discovery is limited to catalogues and provider summaries. Jiji Ghana's [privacy policy](https://jiji.com.gh/privacy.html), effective 14 August 2025, explicitly restricts contact details and chat to registered users. Bark's [Australian Marketplace guidance](https://support.bark.com/hc/en-au/articles/21962063425564-Why-can-t-I-see-the-client-s-contact-details), updated 7 August 2026, keeps provider contacts private by default. These are precedents for controlled contact, not evidence that CEREMO's retention will improve.

Retain customers through saved providers, enquiry/contact history and optional occasion plans, taking relevant precedents from [The Knot's checklist](https://www.theknot.com/wedding-checklist) and [Zola's marketplace workflow](https://www.zola.com/faq/how-do-i-use-zolas-marketplace). Review registration completion alongside contact abandonment, useful connections, return for another service and provider renewal. A saved number remains usable outside CEREMO. No Ghanaian retention uplift has been measured. The [research register](../.github/research/product/customer-contact-retention.md) records alternatives, limits and technical evidence; [ADR 11.10](03-technical-requirements.md#1110-registered-profile-and-contact-access) owns the access decision.

CEREMO must help customers find suitable providers and help providers earn enough useful business to renew a subscription. Customers and providers may exchange contacts, use WhatsApp, meet, agree terms and pay for services outside CEREMO. Those are supported outcomes of discovery.

A customer who finds a photographer and contacts that photographer directly next time may have received good value from CEREMO. Customer retention therefore includes returning for another service, another occasion or a referral. It does not require every conversation or service payment to return to CEREMO.

Provider retention is a separate commercial question: does the listing deliver relevant enquiries and useful visibility at a cost the provider accepts? A household without another occasion has not necessarily rejected CEREMO. A provider receiving many clicks but no suitable enquiries has not necessarily received value.

### Find suitable providers with clear information

Profiles should explain services, service areas, portfolio, current contact details and the meaning of any published price. An indicative starting price is not a final quote. A date preference is not confirmed availability.

Test whether customers can distinguish an assessed provider, a sponsored result and a customer review. Paid tiers may include verification assessment and labelled sponsored placement. Payment cannot buy a passing assessment, favourable stars or removal of a valid complaint.

The review evidence policy remains proposed. External service payments mean CEREMO cannot depend on its own checkout record. Test proportionate evidence, source labels, a provider response and moderation/appeal rules before claiming reviews are verified. Do not default search to five-star providers or hide lower ratings merely because a customer has not selected a filter.

### Help with the whole occasion

An organiser who finds catering may also need sound, seating or photography. Offer relevant categories without requiring a package or assuming that a stated purpose fixes every service they need.

For later occasions, let customers find a previous provider or discover someone else. A saved shortlist can be useful where the customer chooses an account, but account creation should not be necessary to browse. Reminders require consent and an easy way to stop them; sensitive occasions need care.

### Give providers evidence of value

Providers need relevant enquiries, accurate profiles and understandable performance reports. Show views, contact actions and submitted enquiries as different measures. Follow-up can reveal unsuitable locations, dates, budgets, unavailable providers, spam and unanswered enquiries.

A provider subscription may include listing features, verification assessment and labelled sponsored visibility. Detailed tier entitlements remain `null`. Separate verification, promotion or enquiry fees are not approved. The provider should understand what remains available when a trial or paid period ends; research should test the explanation in the [journey document](04-app-flow-and-user-journeys.md).

### Make referrals and return visits useful

Measure a referral when it produces a relevant enquiry, and separately record any later reported agreement. Recommendation intent is not a referral result. Returning to browse for another category or occasion can be useful even when the customer continues dealing directly with a previous provider.

Rewards, wallets and customer memberships are not required by this model. Any future promotion needs an identified payer, cost limit and evidence of additional value. It must not be presented as an approved MVP benefit.

## 14. Financial assessment

No defensible Ghana-wide ceremony-service market size was established. Population, tourism receipts, festival attendance and total Mobile Money turnover do not measure CEREMO's potential bookings.

Use subscription economics rather than service-payment volume:

```text
Provider value = estimated additional gross profit from CEREMO customers
               + attributable time savings
               - subscription cost - enquiry response/admin cost

CEREMO subscription contribution = earned subscription revenue
                                 - collection costs - variable hosting/messages
                                 - support - assessment delivery costs

Cohort contribution = total subscription contribution
                    - provider acquisition/onboarding
                    - allocated customer acquisition and retention spending
```

Keep fixed operating expenses visible and avoid counting a cost twice. Trials are not paying providers. External customer-to-provider service payments are not CEREMO revenue. Attributed business is not automatically additional business; the provider may have won the same customer elsewhere.

### Market sizing and revenue assumptions

Estimate the addressable provider base from a deduplicated count of eligible businesses in supported Greater Accra category/locality segments. A business offering several services counts once as a potential subscriber unless an approved plan explicitly prices otherwise.

| Model input | Evidence required | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible provider base | A documented sampling/counting method, category coverage and deduplication. | Not established. |
| Reachable providers | Acquisition channels, onboarding capacity and actual cost. | Not measured. |
| Trial-to-paid and renewal | Cohorts observed through a disclosed offer and a defined follow-up window. | Not measured. |
| Earned revenue per provider | Approved price, discounts, paid period and finance/tax treatment. | Price and terms unapproved. |
| Contribution and cash need | Collection, assessment, support, acquisition, hosting and fixed operating costs. | No approved budget or forecast. |

A scenario can calculate eligible providers × reachable share × paid conversion × period price, with renewal modelled separately. Until inputs have evidence, this is a sensitivity model rather than a revenue forecast or defensible market-size claim. Population, event attendance and MoMo turnover must not be substituted for paying-provider demand.

## 15. Pilot measures and follow-up

Retain the proposed pilot target of **30 qualified enquiries**, across at least three occasion groups and four service categories. This is a learning target, not a completed-booking requirement or evidence of demand already achieved. A small pilot cannot establish a population retention rate or statistical superiority between plans.

For this pilot, a qualified enquiry is a genuine request to an identified provider about a service relevant to that provider's offering and service area, with enough context to respond. Record missing information and qualification reasons. An exact date or budget may be unknown; do not force completion of the optional guide to reach the target. Contact clicks without a submitted enquiry or a consented report of a genuine request stay separate.

| Measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Relevant enquiries | Qualified enquiries by category, locality and provider; show unsuitable, duplicate, spam and unclassified cases separately |
| Provider response | Acknowledgement and substantive response times, with unanswered cases visible |
| Trial-to-paid conversion | Trial providers making their first verified CEREMO subscription payment divided by the eligible trial cohort; show pending decisions separately |
| Paid renewal | Providers paying for their next period divided by those whose paid period became due; show early, late, unpaid and cancelled cases |
| Enquiry outcomes | Source-labelled reports of conversations, meetings, agreements, decisions not to proceed and unknown outcomes |
| Cross-category use | Customers making another relevant enquiry for a different category within the same occasion |
| Next-occasion return | Previous customers making a new relevant enquiry among those known to have another suitable occasion; report unknown eligibility |
| Referrals | Relevant enquiries attributed to a referral, with later reported outcomes separate |
| Provider value and contribution | Subscription cost, provider-reported work won and the corresponding acquisition/support costs, with attribution limits stated |

Set each test's comparison, terms, follow-up window and decision rule before starting. Define the commercial success thresholds after discovery; none are approved in this report. Daily app opens and free-trial logins are weak main measures for occasional discovery.

### Enquiry outcome records

Use the enquiry states `NEW`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `CLOSED` and `SPAM`. Acknowledgement is not acceptance of a service contract. Closing an enquiry is not proof that the customer hired or paid the provider.

Keep outcomes in separate follow-up reports. Record what was reported, who reported it, when, and whether the other party supplied an independent report. Distinguish customer-reported, provider-reported, both-party reports and unknown outcomes. Preserve disagreements. Even a reported service agreement or payment must not be labelled a CEREMO-verified booking.

For example, a provider may report that a catering enquiry led to an agreement while the customer has not replied to follow-up. Retain that source label. Do not infer a successful agreement from a WhatsApp click, an enquiry closure or silence.

Collect only the details needed for the research and approved service. Private WhatsApp exports, contact books, MoMo PINs and OTPs are unnecessary. Profiles, reviews and complaints still require moderation, investigation and a way to challenge a decision. CEREMO does not promise booking, payment, refund or replacement guarantees.

## 16. Field research and pilot

### Decisions to support

Find out whether CEREMO helps customers reach suitable providers and gives providers enough useful demand to pay and renew. Study real discovery, consultation and payment practices without requiring those activities to move inside CEREMO.

| Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Why choose CEREMO over referrals or social media? | A useful shortlist/contact compared with the normal process |
| Does the guide help? | Task completion, relevance, hesitation and abandonment compared with immediate browsing |
| What makes an enquiry useful? | Service/area fit, customer intent, enough context and actual provider response |
| What does a customer understand from a badge or stars? | Their interpretation of checks, sponsorship, average and review count |
| Will providers subscribe? | A real paid choice after trial, costs, enquiries received and reasons for refusal |
| Will they renew? | Actual renewal, useful demand and competing acquisition options |

Do not treat survey enthusiasm, trial registration, views or contact clicks as paid demand. Do not begin by assuming all Ghanaian customers prefer one channel or distrust all online services.

### Hypotheses and decision protocol

| Hypothesis | Test and comparison | Evidence that changes the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CEREMO improves provider selection | Observe the same kind of service-sourcing task using the organiser's normal approach and proposed CEREMO experience; record assistance and limitations. | Suitable candidates/contact, elapsed effort, information gaps and reasons the ordinary approach remains preferable. |
| The optional guide improves relevance | Compare immediate browsing with the guide, including skipped/unknown answers. | A more useful choice without intrusive questions or avoidable abandonment. Survey completion alone is insufficient. |
| Providers receive commercially useful demand | Follow relevant enquiries, response effort and source-labelled outcomes during the trial. | Provider value after handling/acquisition costs, including unsuitable requests and non-response. |
| The offer supports renewal | Observe an actual purchase and later renewal decision with terms known in advance. | Verified collections, refusal/cancellation reasons and contribution; no inferred sales from clicks. |

Before each round, approve its comparison, recruitment mix, observation window, measures, consent and decision rule. Use the PRD's problem, solution, readiness and commercial gates. Numeric success thresholds remain open; a small purposive sample cannot establish population conversion rates.

### People and methods

Retain the proposed discovery sample of 20 organisers and 20 providers in Greater Accra, recruited in rounds. This purposive sample supports learning; it is not representative of the whole population or every occasion category.

Include recent organisers, people currently planning and people who abandoned or changed an enquiry. Cover family, funeral/memorial, birthday, community/faith, festival and business occasions. Include shared planning arrangements; the person finding the provider may not approve or pay.

Include different service categories, business sizes and ways of operating: calls, notebooks, WhatsApp, websites and business software. Seek providers sceptical of subscriptions as well as interested ones. Vary locality, budget, preferred language, age, gender, device access and digital confidence. Record gaps rather than claiming complete coverage.

Recruit through several independent routes, including offline routes. A Ghana mobile-phone study found demographic selection bias, which supports avoiding recruitment solely through one provider's followers or one social group. It supplies no quotas for this study. [L'Engle et al., 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0190902).

Use a researcher able to interview neutrally, observe normal work, communicate in the participant's preferred language with support where needed, and separate evidence from interpretation. A founder can participate without selling during the interview. Where practical use an interviewer and a note-taker. Ask about actual cases, not only hypothetical willingness. [Interview methods](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews).

Agree appropriate timing, especially for sensitive occasions. Compensation, if offered, pays for time and does not depend on liking or buying CEREMO; no amount is set.

### Interview one actual case

Allow roughly 45–75 minutes, adjusted to the participant. Begin with what happened, then introduce product examples after understanding the usual process.

Ask organisers:

1. Tell me about the last occasion where you helped choose a provider. What was your role?
2. Where did you start? Who recommended the providers and which alternatives did you consider?
3. Who chose, approved and paid? Were those different people?
4. Walk me through the first enquiry. What information did you need before contacting anyone?
5. When did you call, use WhatsApp or meet? What did that help you do?
6. What did you know about services, area, price and quality before making contact?
7. What did you need to believe before paying? What evidence or person helped?
8. What changed after the first conversation? How did you agree the change?
9. What was difficult or disappointing, and how was it handled?
10. Have you used that provider again, needed another category or referred someone? Walk through a real case.
11. Have you had another occasion since? How did you find its services?
12. Which parts of the existing process would you keep?

Ask providers:

1. Walk me through the last suitable enquiry, including how it ended.
2. How did the customer find you? Would they probably have found you another way?
3. What makes an enquiry useful or a waste of time? Show a selected example if possible.
4. What did you need to know about area, date, budget and scope before responding?
5. Why did you choose a particular contact channel or meeting?
6. How do you normally explain price units, inclusions and payment terms?
7. Tell me about the last customer who did not proceed. What do you know about why?
8. What advertising, listing or software services have you actually paid for?
9. How did you decide whether to continue or cancel those services?
10. What business came from them, how do you know, and what did responding cost?
11. Tell me about a customer complaint or review and how you handled it.
12. What would you need to see during a listing trial before paying afterwards?

Follow up with what happened next, what led to the choice, and whether an example is available. Do not suggest that a badge, five stars or personalisation must solve the problem. Record opinions as opinions, not purchasing evidence.

### Observe and test

Within discovery, aim to observe six organisers and six providers doing a normal search/enquiry task. Watch their own devices and normal tools. Note channel switching, voice notes, information gaps, price confusion, other decision-makers and reasons for not proceeding. Ask permission before any recording or selected evidence inspection. [Contextual observation](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/contextual-research-and-observation).

After discovery, run two proposed rounds with six to eight relevant participants each. Paper examples, sample profiles or manually assisted discovery are enough initially. The [web stack](05-ui-ux-design-brief.md) is selected; screen design and native mobile technology remain open.

| Test | Observe |
| --- | --- |
| Optional guide versus immediate browse | Time to a useful shortlist, abandonment and relevant enquiries; completion of the guide is secondary |
| Category-first versus occasion-first | Comprehension and whether questions feel necessary or intrusive |
| Budget as flexible preference versus filter | Units, unknown budget, quote-based prices and empty results |
| Profile with sponsorship, checks and stars | Whether the customer distinguishes each signal and reads review count |
| New provider and no-match results | Understanding and whether broadening criteria is a deliberate choice |
| Edit/reset and marketing controls | Changed suggestions without unwanted marketing or loss of necessary records |
| Trial/paid offer | Understanding of end date, benefit, charge frequency, cancellation and inactive listing |

A live commercial test must use a clearly described real offer and honour its accepted terms. A stated willingness to pay is weaker than a paid subscription and renewal. Do not promise a number of jobs, service-payment protection or a first-place ranking.

### Consent and records

Explain activities, collected information, purpose, audience, optional recording, access/deletion schedule and how to stop or withdraw. Separate recruitment contact details from notes and restrict access. Research participation and marketing permission are different. [Informed consent](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/getting-users-consent-for-research).

Inspect only selected, redacted quotes, receipts or exchanges when useful and permitted. Do not collect whole WhatsApp exports, contact books, identity-document copies, PINs, OTPs or unrelated messages. Obtain consent from another person joining the session. Avoid unnecessary details of bereavement or family relationships.

Use a simple findings record: participant ID, context, observed action, reported experience, selected evidence, interpretation, alternative explanation, product implication and remaining uncertainty. Do not convert notes into invented quotations. Mark hypothetical examples explicitly. Publish anonymised findings; identifiable material needs specific permission.

This research process does not replace the Ghana privacy requirements described in the [original research](01-ghana-research.md).

### Outputs

- Actual discovery/contact journeys and the role of referrals, social media and direct conversation.
- An optional guide tested for usefulness, sensitivity and comprehension.
- A definition of relevant enquiries and honest source-labelled follow-up.
- Evidence that customers distinguish verification, sponsorship and ratings.
- Trial experience, real provider purchase/renewal decisions and associated costs.
- Findings from rejected searches, sceptical providers and unresolved complaints.
- Recommendations for subscription prices, trial duration, tier benefits and review policy, with uncertainties visible.

No external recruitment or interviews have been carried out by creating this plan. Its purpose is to prepare field work, not claim results.

### Commercial and operational risks

| Risk | What would expose it | Response before expansion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weak supply in a needed category/area | Empty searches, too few suitable candidates or dependence on one provider. | Recruit/assess relevant supply and disclose gaps instead of filling results with unrelated subscribers. |
| Little improvement over existing channels | Organisers still need the same search effort or cannot understand profile evidence. | Revise the comparison/contact experience and retest the problem. |
| Providers do not renew | Low-value enquiries, handling costs, refusal or cancellations. | Investigate segment-level value, price and costs before increasing acquisition spend. |
| Trust claims are misunderstood | Users interpret payment, a check or stars as guaranteed quality/refunds. | Correct the claim and validate comprehension; keep moderation independent of subscriptions. |
| Harmful information handling | Overcollection, unauthorised sharing or evidence complaints. | Resolve the affected processing/policy and access controls before further exposure. |
| Incomplete operations or money controls | Billing uncertainty, unanswered cases or failed recovery drills. | Limit live activity to demonstrated support/reconciliation capacity. |

Owners and thresholds require founder/product/engineering review. No funding amount, launch date or financial return is implied by these proposals.

## 18. Account ownership and the first business module

The account is a person; the provider is a business with its own listing, members and subscription. This separation lets a provider owner organise a personal occasion without creating another identity, and lets a business change authorised members without transferring private customer records.

### Existing applications and what they establish

The 20 October review used [Airbnb account switching](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/help/article/3546) and [Sharetribe user types](https://www.sharetribe.com/help/en/articles/9495315-setup-tutorial-1-add-or-edit-user-types) as product precedents for one person acting in more than one capacity. These are dated product observations, not evidence of their databases, frameworks or CEREMO's delivery order.

### Proposed CEREMO workflows and rules

| Responsibility | Product rule |
| --- | --- |
| Person/account | Keycloak authenticates; CEREMO maps the trusted issuer/subject to local status and accepted terms. A profile read does not create or reactivate an account. |
| Provider business | Membership grants defined access to that provider only. Choosing a provider role does not create staff rights. |
| Subscription | Entitlement controls the purchased listing benefits, independently of personal account ownership and check/review outcomes. |
| Re-entry | A previous organiser can return for another service; a provider owner can also act as a customer. |
| Restricted account | Suspension/recovery/deletion decisions follow the applicable account policy, rather than being bypassed by another login or payment. |

On 4 November 2026, the founder approved activation from the boolean verified-email claim in validated Keycloak authentication. Registration remains PENDING without that claim; verified registration or a later explicit activation request may become ACTIVE. Reads do not activate and restricted accounts cannot self-reactivate. Recovery, contact checks and provider ownership/closure policy remain open. See [the activation decision](03-technical-requirements.md#117-account-activation-from-verified-keycloak-email).
